# ADR-0016: Bounded-Target Replay/Freshness Posture

## Status

Proposed

## Context

An attempted milestone acceptance review of [ADR-0013](0013-execution-lifecycle-semantics.md) (execution-lifecycle semantics), [ADR-0014](0014-minimum-execution-evidence-semantics.md) (minimum execution-evidence semantics), and [ADR-0015](0015-first-bounded-execution-target.md) (first bounded execution target) — all three still `Proposed` — surfaced an architectural ambiguity none of the three, individually, was scoped to resolve. [ADR-0012](0012-authorization-to-execution-binding.md) — Accepted — resolved Gate 1's same-process binding mechanism and narrowed Gate 1's replay/freshness requirement to a **process-local single-consumption guarantee**, while stating repeatedly and explicitly that broader replay/freshness properties — wall-clock expiry, policy-reload invalidation, restart-surviving replay prevention, retry/idempotency behavior, and any replay database — "remain open," and that whether they are "eventually resolved by a Gate 2 execution-lifecycle specification, a dedicated Gate 1 addendum, or another future architecture decision is not decided here." ADR-0013's own Decision 10 ("Replay and Freshness — Bounded Resolution") narrowed that openness further, but only along one axis (attempt correlation for retries), and stated with equal explicitness that it does "not present it as resolving Gate 1's broader requirement" and does "not itself constitute the 'dedicated future architecture decision' ADR-0012 anticipated for the remainder." ADR-0015's own "Replay/Freshness Boundary" section restates the same openness a third time, for the specific target it proposes: "Broader replay/freshness properties... remain exactly as open as ADR-0012 leaves them... This ADR's target selection does not narrow, resolve, or presuppose an answer to any of them."

None of ADR-0012, ADR-0013, or ADR-0015 states — because none of them was scoped to ask — whether that continued openness is itself a blocking condition on the specific target ADR-0015 proposes, or whether the target's own constraints mean the broader properties those three decisions all leave open are simply not in play for it. A milestone acceptance review needs an answer to that question before it can respond to ADR-0015's own "Gate 4 Assessment" section, which resolves only Gate 4's applicability, and before it can treat the "replay/freshness" line item in ADR-0015's Consequences and Validation / Implementation Gate sections as fully accounted for one way or the other. This ADR proposes to close exactly that gap, and no more of it.

This ADR treats one part of that open list with more precision than ADR-0012's original phrasing invites. ADR-0012 named "retry/idempotency behavior" as a single, unbundled item among the broader properties left open. That label folds together two distinct concerns: whether a retry may reuse or replay a prior authorization disposition or binding record instead of earning its own (an **authorization-replay** concern), and whether a retry can cause the same real-world operation to be dispatched twice when a prior attempt's outcome was never confirmed (an **operational duplicate-execution / idempotency** concern). This ADR proposes to answer only the first. It does not treat the second as resolved by anything ADR-0012 or ADR-0013 already provides, and it says so explicitly below (**Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency**).

[`docs/architecture/execution-boundary-discovery-assessment.md`](../architecture/execution-boundary-discovery-assessment.md) §7 ("Replay and Freshness") is the evidentiary basis both ADR-0012 and this ADR reason from. Findings from §7 this ADR relies on directly, restated rather than re-derived: "a same-process topology at least has the option of tracking 'have I already dispatched the operation this specific `AuditEvidence` authorized' as simple in-memory state, without needing a distributed mechanism"; "does a network handoff to a separate executor require stronger replay controls? Yes — this follows directly from [the] finding that separated topologies reopen the confused-deputy risk... a strictly worse replay surface than same-process reuse of a disposition the process already holds honestly"; and, on policy-reload invalidation specifically, "in a same-process topology there is no 'later' to re-check against unless the process deliberately introduces a delay between disposition and dispatch." This last finding is the hinge this ADR's causal analysis turns on — read precisely, it is a claim about what the architecture does and does not deliberately introduce, not a claim that zero time separates disposition from dispatch.

Consistent with this repository's ADR-acceptance governance convention (ADR-0007 through ADR-0015 were each first merged with `Status: Proposed`, and a separate, dedicated follow-up PR later changed the status to `Accepted` for those that have since been accepted — see [`docs/adr/README.md`](README.md#lifecycle-states)), this ADR is submitted as `Proposed`. Nothing in this ADR, and nothing in its merging, constitutes acceptance. This ADR does not perform the ADR-0013/ADR-0014/ADR-0015 milestone acceptance review itself, does not change any of their statuses, and does not resolve Gate 2 or Gate 3. The current implemented state remains exactly what ADR-0011 through ADR-0015 all describe: no protocol operation is executed anywhere in the ecosystem today, under any disposition, and this ADR's proposal — let alone its eventual acceptance — does not change that.

## Problem

For the exact bounded topology ADR-0015 proposes — same-process execution colocated with `basis-producer` (ADR-0011 Decision 3); a deterministic local test service or equivalent controlled reference endpoint, never a real production endpoint, physical device, or cloud service; no protocol/device credential of any kind; one tightly bounded REST operation shape; no remotely transferable execution-authorization artifact; ADR-0012's process-local single-consumption binding; and ADR-0013's proposed retry/attempt-correlation semantics — is ADR-0012's binding, together with ADR-0013's lifecycle/retry semantics, sufficient to allow a future bounded reference implementation to proceed without first solving generalized replay/freshness? Or does this exact target depend on some additional, currently undecided replay/freshness property, and if so, what is the smallest such property?

The answer must not reason "broader replay/freshness remains open, therefore implementation is blocked" — that conflates a global open question with a local dependency question, and ADR-0012 and ADR-0015 already establish that the two are not the same thing. It must not reason "the target is small, therefore replay/freshness does not matter" — that substitutes an intuition about scale for an analysis of mechanism. The answer must instead identify what failure mode each broader replay/freshness property exists to prevent, and determine, mechanism by mechanism, whether that failure mode can actually occur inside the exact topology ADR-0015 constrains, given what ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 already provide.

This ADR is scoped to **authorization replay/freshness**: whether a disposition or binding artifact can be reused, replayed, or acted on after it should no longer be valid. It is not scoped to, and does not attempt to answer, whether every operational retry hazard is already solved — in particular, whether a freshly and validly re-authorized retry can still duplicate a real-world effect from a first attempt whose outcome was never confirmed. That is a distinct question, addressed on its own terms below (**Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency**), not folded into this ADR's conclusion by default.

## Decision

**For the exact bounded topology ADR-0015 proposes, ADR-0012's process-local single-consumption binding, together with ADR-0013's proposed retry/attempt-correlation semantics, is sufficient: this specific bounded target does not depend on broader authorization replay/freshness — wall-clock expiry, policy-reload invalidation, restart-surviving replay prevention, or a replay database — before a bounded reference implementation may be planned against it.** This conclusion is scoped to authorization replay/freshness specifically. It does not extend to, and this ADR does not claim to resolve, operational duplicate-execution/idempotency risk — whether a freshly and validly authorized retry can still cause the same real-world operation to be dispatched twice after a prior attempt's outcome went unconfirmed. That question is treated separately, below, and nothing in this Decision should be read as resolving it.

This conclusion is reached by determining, for each broader authorization-replay property, what failure mode it exists to prevent and whether that failure mode has a mechanism to occur within this topology's own constraints — not by inference from the target's size, and not by inference from the fact that broader replay/freshness remains globally unresolved. Broader replay/freshness remains exactly as open, globally, as ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 leave it. This ADR does not resolve it, does not design any part of it, and does not claim to be the "dedicated future architecture decision" both ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 reserve for it. It decides only that the specific topology named below does not depend on that decision arriving first, for the authorization-replay portion of what remains open.

### What Broader Replay/Freshness Protects Against

Each property ADR-0012 named as open exists to close a specific failure mode. Naming the failure mode, not the property's label, is the analytical unit this decision applies. "Retry/idempotency behavior" is split into its two component failure modes rather than treated as one, per **Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency**, below:

- **Wall-clock expiry** exists to prevent a disposition from being acted on so long after it was issued that the conditions under which it was authorized can no longer be assumed to hold. The failure mode requires a disposition to be *held*, unconsumed, for an interval long enough that staleness becomes plausible.
- **Policy-reload invalidation** exists to prevent dispatch under a disposition whose authorizing policy state has since changed — a bundle reload between decision and dispatch that the pending disposition does not know about. The failure mode requires a nonzero, architecturally exploitable window between decision and dispatch during which a reload could occur and matter.
- **Restart-surviving replay prevention** exists to prevent a persisted binding or disposition artifact from being replayed after the process that created it has stopped and restarted. The failure mode requires an artifact that survives the process's own lifetime — something a restart does not erase.
- **Retry/idempotency behavior — authorization-facing half.** Exists to prevent a second dispatch attempt from proceeding by reusing or replaying a prior authorization disposition or binding record, rather than earning its own. The failure mode requires a retry path that does not re-earn its own authorization.
- **Retry/idempotency behavior — operational half.** Exists to bound how many times, and under what conditions, the same logical real-world effect may legitimately be dispatched when a prior attempt's outcome was uncertain or unconfirmed, so that a retry does not silently duplicate an effect that already occurred. This failure mode is independent of whether each dispatch was separately, validly authorized: fresh authorization answers "was this dispatch currently permitted," not "has this operation's effect already happened." This is the half this ADR does **not** resolve — see **Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency**, below.
- **A replay database** exists to detect reuse of a portable or persisted artifact across processes, restarts, or network positions. The failure mode requires such an artifact to exist for a database to check against.

Every failure mode this ADR resolves is a property of *authorization artifacts that outlive, or travel beyond, the single act they were created for* — a disposition held across a delay, a binding record surviving a restart, an artifact crossing a process or network boundary, a retry that reuses rather than re-earns authorization. None of them is a property of scale, and this ADR does not treat target size as evidence either way. The operational-duplicate-execution failure mode is not a property of artifact lifetime at all, which is exactly why it is treated separately rather than folded into this list's conclusion.

### Why the ADR-0015 Bounded Topology Does Not Create Any of Those Authorization-Replay Artifacts

Each constraint below is drawn from ADR-0011, ADR-0012, ADR-0013, and ADR-0015 as accepted or proposed, not asserted independently by this ADR. This section addresses the authorization-replay failure modes only:

- **No architecture-defined mechanism holds an unconsumed disposition.** ADR-0011 Decision 9 prohibits "speculative execution, pre-dispatch, optimistic dispatch, or asynchronous 'authorize while executing' behavior," and states that "execution must never begin before the authoritative disposition permitting execution has been received and validated." ADR-0011 Decision 3's own conceptual pipeline (`ProtocolOperation → producer authorization pipeline → authoritative ALLOW → authorization-to-execution binding verification → same-process logical protocol executor → protocol endpoint`) and ADR-0012's requirement that binding verification is "deliberately colocated with dispatch itself" describe a single, synchronous, in-process call sequence, not a queue, a scheduler, or a store where a disposition waits. Nothing in ADR-0011, ADR-0012, or ADR-0015 authorizes introducing one for this target. Without an architecture-defined mechanism that holds a disposition open for later, unconsumed use, wall-clock expiry has no architecturally introduced interval whose staleness this topology would need to bound — whatever time does elapse is the intrinsic duration of one synchronous call sequence, not an externally held or extendable interval.
- **No architecture-defined decision-to-dispatch window.** The discovery assessment's own finding is direct on this point: "in a same-process topology there is no 'later' to re-check against unless the process deliberately introduces a delay between disposition and dispatch." This bounded target's architecture introduces no queue, scheduler, or deliberate hold mechanism between disposition and dispatch — the same absence of a queue or hold mechanism that closes the wall-clock-expiry failure mode closes this one for the same reason. Policy-reload invalidation is a real concern for a topology whose architecture allows a meaningful decision-to-dispatch gap to be introduced; this topology, as constrained, defines no such gap. This is a claim about what the architecture authorizes and colocates, not a claim that literally zero time elapses between disposition and dispatch — some non-zero interval necessarily exists for the synchronous call sequence itself to execute. The distinction that matters architecturally is that this interval is not held open, extendable, or externally controllable by a queue, a scheduler, or a deliberate delay; it is the intrinsic duration of one in-process call, not a window a bug, an attacker, or a delayed caller can widen.
- **No artifact that survives the process.** Accepted ADR-0012 establishes two distinct facts this ADR keeps separate, rather than collapsing into one overstated claim: the binding mechanism is same-process-local — the binding record is never "submitted to `basis-gateway`" and does not "appear on the operation-aware request or response" — and the **process-local single-consumption guarantee** it enforces "does not survive a process restart, and provides no protection against a deliberately compromised process replaying its own state" (ADR-0012's Freshness and single use section). That sentence's subject is the *guarantee*, not the binding record as an artifact; ADR-0012 does not itself make a normative claim that the record is guaranteed erased or unrecoverable on restart, only that the guarantee's enforcement does not extend across one. **This ADR does not rely on that ADR-0012 sentence for more than it states. It instead adds its own, independent bounded assumption, sufficient on its own for this conclusion: no binding record, disposition, or equivalent authorization artifact is persisted beyond the creating process's own running lifetime, or transmitted outside that process (The Boundary of This Conclusion, below).** Under that bounded assumption, a restart leaves no persisted binding record behind for an attacker or a bug to replay. There is no persistence layer anywhere in ADR-0011's, ADR-0012's, or ADR-0015's description of this target for such a record to survive in.
- **No transferable artifact.** ADR-0012 considered and rejected, for this same-process topology, "a signed execution grant, JWT, nonce, TTL, or one-time-use token," finding that "cryptographic protection is necessary only when the channel carrying the bound value is not already trusted by some other means; in the same-process topology, the channel is process memory." ADR-0015's target inherits this unchanged: no credential, no portable authorization artifact, and — per this ADR's own bounding assumptions, below — no artifact designed to be carried outside the process that created it. A replay database exists to catch reuse of exactly the kind of artifact this topology does not produce.
- **Retries re-earn authorization; they do not reuse it — for the authorization-facing half only.** ADR-0012's single-consumption guarantee means a binding record, once consumed, cannot authorize a second dispatch. ADR-0013's Decision 9 (proposed) makes the consequence explicit: "any dispatch action following a prior attempt's terminal outcome... is a new, distinct execution attempt... because ADR-0012's binding record is single-consumption, no second dispatch can occur under the *same* binding once the first has consumed it — a retry, if one occurs at all, necessarily requires the operation to be re-authorized and re-bound." A retry under this topology is not a replay of the first attempt's authorization; it is a fresh pass through `basis-core` and `basis-gateway` against whatever policy state holds at retry time. This closes the authorization-facing half of the retry/idempotency failure mode named above: a second dispatch cannot proceed by reusing a prior, already-consumed binding. **It does not, by itself, close the operational half** — see **Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency**, below, for why fresh authorization does not also answer whether the same effect was already dispatched once.

### Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency

ADR-0012's original list of open, broader properties named "retry/idempotency behavior" as a single item. That label bundles two conceptually distinct failure modes, and this ADR treats them separately rather than inheriting the bundling.

**Authorization replay / binding reuse** — whether a second dispatch can proceed by reusing, replaying, or resuming a prior authorization disposition or binding record instead of earning its own. ADR-0012's single-consumption guarantee and ADR-0013 Decision 9 (proposed) close this failure mode for this topology: a consumed binding record cannot authorize a second dispatch, and any subsequent dispatch attempt requires its own fresh `ALLOW` and its own fresh, single-use binding, re-evaluated against whatever policy state holds at that later moment. This is the failure mode the mechanism-by-mechanism analysis above actually establishes, and the only one this ADR's Outcome A conclusion rests on.

**Operational duplicate-execution / idempotency** — whether dispatching a second, freshly and validly authorized attempt for the same logical operation can cause the underlying real-world (or, for this target, test-fixture) effect to occur twice, when a prior attempt's outcome was never confirmed. This is a materially different question from authorization replay, and fresh authorization does not answer it: re-authorization at `basis-core` and `basis-gateway` evaluates whether the requested operation is currently permitted under policy — it has no visibility into, and no mechanism for tracking, whether an operation with the same intended effect was already dispatched once and left in `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`. A second `ALLOW`, and a second fresh binding, would both be entirely legitimate on their own terms even if the first attempt in fact succeeded silently. **This ADR does not claim that ADR-0012's single-consumption guarantee or ADR-0013's retry/attempt-correlation semantics resolve this — they do not, and neither claims to.** ADR-0013 Decision 10 says so directly: it resolves only "attempt multiplicity... represented as distinct, explicitly correlated attempts," and explicitly defers "whether or how a caller may request a retry, or how many retries are permitted" to a dedicated future architecture decision. Operational duplicate-execution risk is exactly that deferred question, not a new gap this ADR discovers.

**Does this distinction change Outcome A?** No, for the question this ADR is actually scoped to answer, and this ADR states that conclusion directly rather than deferring it. The question this ADR proposes to answer is whether the ADR-0015 bounded topology requires broader *authorization* replay/freshness protection — a mechanism to stop a stale, reused, or persisted authorization artifact from being acted on — before a bounded reference implementation may be planned. It is not whether every operational retry hazard is already solved; no ADR in this chain, including ADR-0013, has ever claimed that, and ADR-0012 itself already listed "retry/idempotency behavior" as unresolved without distinguishing its two halves. The two failure modes are independent: closing the authorization-replay half does not require the operational half to be closed too, and leaving the operational half open does not reopen the authorization-replay analysis above, because nothing about an unresolved duplicate-execution hazard makes a consumed binding record replayable or gives a stale disposition a way to be acted on again. Outcome A therefore holds for authorization replay/freshness specifically. It would not hold, and this ADR would need to be re-reasoned rather than merely re-worded, if operational duplicate-execution risk turned out to require a mechanism that also touched the authorization boundary itself (for example, a shared replay ledger spanning both concerns) — this ADR's own analysis finds no such shared mechanism is required to answer the question it was asked.

**This does not mean operational duplicate-execution risk is immaterial for the ADR-0015 target, or that this ADR resolves it by omission.** It remains genuinely open, is not addressed by this ADR, and this ADR does not invent idempotency architecture — a caller-facing retry policy, an idempotency key, or operation-level deduplication — to close it. The primary reason this ADR does not treat that openness as a blocker to the narrower question it answers is a constraint on the bounded implementation itself, not a property of the target's safety profile: **the first bounded reference implementation performs exactly one dispatch attempt per authorization-to-execution cycle — authorize → bind → dispatch → observe → record lifecycle/evidence → stop — and must not include automatic retry, automatic redispatch, caller-transparent retry orchestration, or any other behavior that decides, on its own, whether an `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or other uncertain prior attempt should be dispatched again.** Because the bounded implementation never initiates a second dispatch by itself, the specific hazard operational duplicate-execution/idempotency exists to guard against — a system silently redispatching an operation whose first outcome was never confirmed — has no occasion to arise inside the bounded slice as this ADR constrains it. If another dispatch is ever initiated for the same logical operation, ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 already require it to be a distinct attempt with its own fresh authorization and its own fresh binding (**Authorization replay / binding reuse**, above); this ADR does not decide, and does not attempt to decide, when — or whether — such another attempt should occur. Operational retry policy, a maximum retry count, automatic retry triggers, idempotency keys, deduplication, and duplicate-effect handling all remain open architecture, deferred exactly as ADR-0013 Decision 10 already defers them. The target's credential-free, no-real-device, deterministic-test-service constraints remain relevant as an additional containment characteristic — a duplicate dispatch during architecture validation, should one somehow still occur, carries none of the physical-world consequence ADR-0015's own "no safety exposure" target-selection reasoning already relies on for a different purpose — but this is a secondary, reinforcing point, not the primary reason operational idempotency's openness is non-blocking here. **A future target or a future implementation that introduces automatic retry, automatic redispatch, or caller-transparent retry orchestration — for the ADR-0015 target or any other — would need operational duplicate-execution/idempotency resolved before doing so, independent of this ADR's conclusion, and independent of whether authorization replay/freshness has already been addressed for that topology.**

### The Boundary of This Conclusion

This conclusion — for authorization replay/freshness — holds only for the topology named above, and only for as long as every one of the following continues to hold. It is not a general property of same-process execution, and it is not a property this ADR extends to any topology beyond the one ADR-0015 names. Most of this boundary list governs the authorization-replay conclusion only; the final bullet below additionally states the condition **Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency** (above) depends on for treating operational duplicate-execution/idempotency's continued openness as non-blocking for this bounded target specifically. Neither this list nor that additional bullet bounds, narrows, or resolves operational duplicate-execution/idempotency risk itself, which remains open regardless of whether these constraints hold:

- Execution remains same-process and colocated with `basis-producer`, per ADR-0011 Decision 3.
- No queue, scheduler, durable store, or other mechanism holds a disposition or a binding record between authorization and dispatch; binding verification remains colocated with dispatch, per ADR-0012.
- No binding record, disposition, or equivalent authorization artifact is persisted beyond the creating process's own running lifetime, or transmitted outside that process.
- The target remains credential-free, per ADR-0015's Gate 4 Assessment; no protocol/device credential is introduced.
- The target remains the deterministic local test service or equivalent controlled reference endpoint ADR-0015 names — never a real production endpoint, a physical device, a real OT protocol, or a cloud service.
- A retry, if one occurs, is always a new authorization-to-execution cycle with its own fresh `ALLOW` and its own fresh, single-use binding record, per ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 Decision 9 — never a resumption or reuse of a prior, already-consumed one.
- The bounded reference implementation performs at most one dispatch attempt per authorization-to-execution cycle (authorize → bind → dispatch → observe → record lifecycle/evidence → stop) and does not itself include automatic retry, automatic redispatch, or caller-transparent retry orchestration deciding whether an uncertain prior attempt should be dispatched again.

**Any change to the first six of these — a transferable authorization artifact, a process boundary between authorization and dispatch, network-separated execution, persistence of a binding record or disposition beyond one process's running lifetime, a protocol/device credential, a real OT target, or a deliberately introduced delay or queue between disposition and dispatch — reopens this authorization-replay question.** A future target where a duplicate dispatch would carry a real-world consequence separately reopens the operational-duplicate-execution question, on its own terms, regardless of whether the authorization-replay boundary above still holds. **Separately, a future implementation that introduces automatic retry, automatic redispatch, or caller-transparent retry orchestration reopens the operational-duplicate-execution non-blocking justification specifically (the seventh bullet above and Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency, above), regardless of whether the authorization-replay boundary continues to hold.** This ADR does not evaluate any topology other than the one it names, and its conclusion carries no presumption about how a changed topology, or a changed implementation, would resolve either question.

### What This Decision Does Not Claim

This ADR does not claim that broader replay/freshness is solved, closed, or no longer architecturally significant. It remains exactly as open, globally, as ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 leave it: wall-clock expiry, policy-reload invalidation, restart-surviving replay prevention, operational duplicate-execution/idempotency, and any replay database are all still undecided, for any topology that actually depends on them. This ADR does not decide any of them, does not design a mechanism for any of them, and does not shorten the sequence by which they would eventually be resolved. It states, narrowly, that the specific topology ADR-0015 names does not depend on the authorization-replay portion of that decision arriving before a bounded reference implementation may be planned against it — the bounded topology's own constraints mean the authorization-replay failure modes those broader properties exist to prevent do not have a mechanism to occur inside it, not that BASIS has decided those failure modes, or the separate operational-duplicate-execution failure mode, do not matter elsewhere. This ADR deliberately avoids describing the bounded target as "exempt" from a required security property; the framing throughout is that the property's underlying failure modes do not have a channel to occur in this specific, constrained topology, not that a channel exists and has been excused.

### Dependence on ADR-0013's Proposed Status

ADR-0013 remains `Proposed`, not `Accepted`. This ADR relies on two parts of ADR-0013 as working inputs: Decision 9's retry/attempt-correlation reasoning, for its authorization-replay conclusion, exactly as ADR-0015 already relies on ADR-0013's three-axis lifecycle vocabulary without treating that reliance as an acceptance of ADR-0013; and Decision 10's explicit deferral of retry-policy and idempotency questions, as confirmation that operational duplicate-execution/idempotency is already-acknowledged open architecture rather than a gap this ADR newly introduces. If ADR-0013's retry/attempt-correlation semantics change materially before ADR-0013's own acceptance review, this ADR's authorization-replay conclusion — specifically, the claim that a retry always requires a fresh binding rather than reusing a consumed one — would need reconciliation before this ADR could itself be accepted. Independent of ADR-0013, this ADR's conclusion does not depend on any part of ADR-0013's lifecycle-observation vocabulary (attempt outcome, resulting-state verification, evidence durability) other than Decisions 9 and 10; a change to those other parts of ADR-0013 does not, by itself, require reconciling this ADR.

## Gate Posture

If accepted, this ADR would establish the following gate posture for the pre-implementation architecture gates ADR-0011 established:

- **Gate 1's same-process authorization-to-execution binding mechanism remains resolved by Accepted ADR-0012.** This ADR does not reopen ADR-0012's binding model, its trust assumptions, or its failure semantics.
- **Broader replay/freshness remains an open architectural concern for any topology that depends on it.** This ADR does not narrow that openness for any topology other than the one it names. A future separated-executor topology, a topology introducing persistence or a transferable artifact, or a future real-OT-protocol target must reopen the authorization-replay question this ADR answers here, exactly as ADR-0011 Decision 5 and ADR-0012's own "Future Separated Topology Considerations" section already anticipate.
- **This proposal concludes that the ADR-0015 first bounded target does not depend on the broader authorization-replay/freshness guarantees named above, under the accepted and proposed constraints named above.** It does not, by the same token, resolve operational duplicate-execution/idempotency, which remains open regardless. This proposal treats that openness as non-blocking for this specific bounded target primarily because the bounded implementation itself performs at most one dispatch attempt per authorization cycle and must not automatically retry or redispatch an uncertain outcome — not primarily because the target is a low-consequence test service, which remains only a secondary, reinforcing consideration (**Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency**, above). This is the specific finding this ADR proposes to add to the record, pending its own acceptance.
- **If accepted, this ADR would remove the ambiguity that blocked the attempted ADR-0013/ADR-0014/ADR-0015 milestone acceptance review with respect to authorization replay/freshness specifically:** a reviewer evaluating those three ADRs would no longer need to treat "broader replay/freshness remains open" as an unresolved authorization-level dependency of the target ADR-0015 proposes. It would not resolve any other part of that review, including the separate, still-open operational duplicate-execution/idempotency question. Gate 2 (ADR-0013) and Gate 3 (ADR-0014) remain open exactly as they were before this ADR, pending their own separate, dedicated acceptance reviews, and this ADR does not perform or substitute for either.

This ADR does not mark ADR-0013, ADR-0014, or ADR-0015 `Accepted`. It does not state or imply that implementation may begin because this ADR exists, or because this ADR is accepted. Acceptance of this ADR, by itself, authorizes nothing.

## Alternatives Considered

**A — Conclude that the bounded target depends on broader authorization replay/freshness (Outcome B).** Considered and rejected on the evidence: every broader authorization-replay failure mode requires a mechanism — a held disposition, an architecturally introduced decision-to-dispatch window, a persisted or transferable artifact, or a retry that bypasses re-authorization — and none of those mechanisms exists in the topology ADR-0015 constrains, given ADR-0011 Decision 9's prohibition on speculative/asynchronous dispatch and ADR-0012's same-process, non-persistent, single-consumption binding. Concluding dependence without identifying which specific mechanism creates the exposure would not be a security-motivated conclusion; it would be an unsupported one.

**B — Conclude "the target is small, therefore replay/freshness does not matter."** Rejected as the specific reasoning error this ADR's own Problem section rules out. Target size is not a security property; a small, credential-free target could still depend on broader authorization replay/freshness if its topology introduced persistence, a transferable artifact, or an architecturally introduced decision-to-dispatch delay. This ADR's conclusion rests on the absence of those mechanisms, not on the target's scope being narrow.

**C — Conclude "broader replay/freshness remains open, therefore this target is blocked."** Rejected as the other reasoning error this ADR's Problem section rules out. ADR-0012 and ADR-0015 already establish that a property remaining globally open is not the same fact as a specific topology depending on it; treating global openness as an automatic blocker for every topology would make it impossible for any bounded target, ever, to proceed while any part of replay/freshness remained unresolved anywhere in the architecture — a stronger claim than the evidence supports and one no prior ADR in this chain has made.

**D — Design a nonce, nonce-store, TTL, or persistence mechanism now, defensively, rather than analyze whether one is needed.** Rejected. ADR-0012 already declined a signed execution grant, JWT, nonce, or TTL for this same-process topology on the finding that "cryptographic protection is necessary only when the channel... is not already trusted by some other means" — a finding this ADR's own mechanism-by-mechanism analysis reaches independently rather than merely inheriting. Introducing one now, without a mechanism this ADR's analysis identifies as requiring it, would repeat the "invent crypto for elegance" pattern ADR-0007, ADR-0008, and ADR-0012 already declined, and would exceed this ADR's own scope restriction against designing generalized replay protection.

**E — Defer this question until ADR-0013 and ADR-0014 reach formal acceptance.** Considered, not selected. This ADR's authorization-replay conclusion depends on exactly two pieces of ADR-0013 — Decisions 9 and 10 — not on ADR-0013's full lifecycle vocabulary or on any part of ADR-0014. ADR-0015 already established the precedent that a target-scoped decision may rely on a specific, narrow piece of Proposed architecture as a working input without waiting for that architecture's own acceptance, provided the dependency is stated plainly and reconciliation is required if that piece changes (**Dependence on ADR-0013's Proposed Status**, above). Deferring this narrower question until two much broader ADRs are separately accepted would leave the milestone acceptance review blocked on a dependency this ADR does not actually need resolved first.

**F — Treat operational duplicate-execution/idempotency as already resolved because retries re-earn fresh authorization.** Considered and rejected — this is the specific conflation this ADR's hardening pass exists to correct. Fresh authorization answers whether a dispatch is currently permitted under policy; it does not answer whether the same real-world effect already occurred once under a prior, unconfirmed attempt, because neither `basis-core` nor `basis-gateway` tracks execution history. ADR-0013 Decision 10 itself explicitly declines to resolve retry policy or idempotency; treating the question as solved here would contradict the very ADR this ADR relies on for its authorization-replay conclusion. This ADR instead separates the two questions explicitly (**Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency**, above) and proposes to resolve only the first.

## Consequences

### Positive

- If accepted, this proposal would give a milestone acceptance review a reasoned, evidence-grounded answer to the specific ambiguity that blocked the attempted ADR-0013/ADR-0014/ADR-0015 review — whether the ADR-0015 target's dependence on broader authorization replay/freshness was itself an unresolved blocker — reached by identifying failure modes and checking them against the topology's actual constraints rather than by assuming either extreme.
- If accepted, Gate 1's status for this specific target would become precise: same-process mechanism resolved (ADR-0012, Accepted); broader authorization-replay properties open globally but not depended upon by this target (this ADR); operational duplicate-execution/idempotency separately and explicitly left open rather than silently assumed solved.
- The reasoning method — name the failure mode a property exists to prevent, then check whether the topology has a mechanism for that failure mode to occur — and the authorization-replay/operational-idempotency distinction this ADR draws are both available for reuse by later architecture work evaluating a different topology's replay/freshness posture, without this ADR itself generalizing either conclusion.

### Negative / Tradeoff

- This ADR's conclusion is conditional on a list of topology constraints (**The Boundary of This Conclusion**, above) that a future implementation plan must not silently violate; a future implementer who adds persistence, a queue, or a transferable artifact "for convenience" without recognizing that doing so reopens this question would be acting outside architecture that has actually been decided.
- This ADR's reliance on ADR-0013 Decisions 9 and 10 means a material change to either, before ADR-0013's own acceptance, would require this ADR to be reconciled — a dependency stated plainly rather than hidden, but a dependency nonetheless.
- This ADR proposes to resolve authorization replay/freshness only; it leaves operational duplicate-execution/idempotency exactly as open as ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 already left it. A reader who treats this ADR as having closed "replay/freshness" in general — rather than its authorization-facing half specifically — would be mistaken in exactly the way **Authorization Replay Versus Operational Idempotency** exists to prevent.
- This ADR proposes to resolve a narrower question than "is Gate 1 fully resolved" — it does not accelerate resolution of wall-clock expiry, policy-reload invalidation, restart-surviving replay prevention, operational duplicate-execution/idempotency, or a replay database for any topology that does depend on them, and a reader who mistakes this ADR for that broader resolution would be mistaken.

## Security Consequences

### Prevented by this architecture

- A milestone acceptance review, or a future implementation plan, incorrectly treating the ADR-0015 target as blocked by an undecided global property it does not actually depend on.
- A milestone acceptance review, or a future implementation plan, incorrectly treating the ADR-0015 target's small scope as evidence that replay/freshness has been considered and found unnecessary, without the mechanism-by-mechanism analysis this ADR performs.
- A reader conflating this ADR's authorization-replay conclusion with a claim that operational duplicate-execution is already prevented for this target, when it is not.
- Silent scope creep in which a future implementation adds persistence, a queue, or a transferable artifact to the bounded target without recognizing that doing so reopens the authorization-replay question this ADR proposes to close only for the topology as constrained.

### Still requiring follow-on architecture

- **Operational duplicate-execution/idempotency risk** — whether a freshly and validly authorized retry can duplicate an unconfirmed prior attempt's effect. ADR-0013 Decision 10 already defers this, and this ADR does not resolve it either; it remains open for the ADR-0015 target and for every other topology.
- Wall-clock expiry, policy-reload invalidation, restart-surviving replay prevention, and any replay database — all exactly as open, globally, as ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 leave them, for any topology that depends on them. This ADR does not resolve any of them and does not accelerate their resolution.
- Gate 2 (ADR-0013) and Gate 3 (ADR-0014) acceptance — both remain outstanding regardless of this ADR.
- A portable, authenticated binding artifact and its accompanying replay/freshness architecture, if a separated-executor topology is ever selected (ADR-0011 Decision 5; ADR-0012's "Future Separated Topology Considerations") — this ADR's conclusion does not extend to that topology and does not anticipate its answer.
- Gate 4, if a future target requires a protocol/device credential — unaffected by this ADR, which addresses only the credential-free target ADR-0015 proposes.

### Residual risks

- Independent of any compromise: a non-compromised, correctly functioning implementation of this bounded target can still dispatch the same logical operation twice — once with an unconfirmed (`OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`) outcome and once via a freshly authorized retry — without either dispatch being an authorization-replay violation. This is not a residual risk in the same sense as a compromised-process risk; it is the open operational-duplicate-execution question stated above, restated here for visibility rather than left implicit.
- A fully compromised same-process producer/executor process can fabricate its own bookkeeping — including a false "not yet consumed" state for a binding record, or a false record of having re-authorized before a retry — because the same process both creates and checks that state. This is the same residual-risk structure ADR-0011 Decision 19 and ADR-0012 already accept for a compromised same-process component, restated here rather than newly introduced; no broader authorization-replay mechanism this ADR declines to require would close this gap either, because it is a property of process integrity, not of the freshness or persistence design of an authorization artifact.
- This ADR's conclusion depends on the bounded topology's constraints continuing to hold in whatever implementation is eventually planned against it; nothing in this ADR verifies that a future implementation actually respects those constraints, and this ADR does not create an enforcement mechanism for them.

## Relationship to ADR-0011

This ADR does not reopen ADR-0011's protocol-executor role establishment, its same-process default-topology selection, or its governing invariants. It relies on Decision 3's topology and Decision 9's prohibition on speculative, pipelined, or asynchronous dispatch as load-bearing evidence for its own authorization-replay conclusion — specifically, that this topology's architecture defines no queue, hold, or deliberate-delay mechanism between decision and dispatch for a policy-reload or wall-clock-staleness failure mode to exploit. It does not modify Gate 1 through Gate 4 as ADR-0011 defined them; it states a posture for the currently-open, authorization-replay portion of Gate 1 as applied to one specific target, nothing more.

## Relationship to ADR-0012

This ADR does not reopen ADR-0012's binding model, its same-process trust assumptions, or its failure semantics. It relies directly on ADR-0012's own statements that the binding record and binding mechanism are same-process-local, that the process-local single-consumption guarantee ADR-0012 enforces does not survive a process restart, and that this topology requires no signed grant, nonce, or TTL — treating those as the accepted architecture they are, not re-deriving them. **This ADR keeps that guarantee-level statement distinct from a stronger, record-level claim ADR-0012 does not itself make: that the binding record artifact is guaranteed erased or unrecoverable across a restart.** For that stronger property, this ADR relies instead on its own independent bounded assumption — no binding record, disposition, or equivalent authorization artifact is persisted beyond the creating process's own running lifetime, or transmitted outside it (**The Boundary of This Conclusion**, above) — which is sufficient on its own for this ADR's conclusion regardless of what ADR-0012 does or does not additionally guarantee at the record level. If accepted, this ADR would be the closest thing to the "dedicated future architecture decision" ADR-0012 anticipated for its remaining open replay/freshness question, but only for the one topology it evaluates and only for that question's authorization-facing half; it explicitly does not claim to be that decision for operational duplicate-execution/idempotency, for any other topology, or for the broader properties in general.

## Relationship to ADR-0013

This ADR depends on two parts of ADR-0013 as working, not-yet-accepted inputs, per **Dependence on ADR-0013's Proposed Status**, above: Decision 9's retry/attempt-correlation reasoning, for its authorization-replay conclusion, and Decision 10's explicit deferral of retry-policy and idempotency questions, as confirmation that operational duplicate-execution/idempotency is already-acknowledged open architecture rather than a gap this ADR introduces. It does not depend on, evaluate, or reopen ADR-0013's three-axis lifecycle vocabulary (attempt outcome, resulting-state verification, evidence durability), its fail-closed requirements, or its multi-stage protocol-operation handling. It does not resolve Gate 2, does not resolve operational duplicate-execution/idempotency, and does not change ADR-0013's status.

## Relationship to ADR-0014

This ADR does not depend on, evaluate, or reopen any part of ADR-0014. Execution-evidence construction, retention, correlation, and the evidence-durability axis remain exactly as ADR-0014 proposes them, unaffected by this ADR's conclusion. It does not resolve Gate 3 and does not change ADR-0014's status.

## Relationship to ADR-0015

This ADR evaluates exactly the target ADR-0015 proposes and no other; **it does not select that target itself** — ADR-0015 does, in its own **Target Selection Criteria** — and this ADR does not reopen ADR-0015's target selection, its Gate 4 Assessment, or its Schema Publication Boundary. **This ADR's evaluation is only as meaningful, for implementation-readiness purposes, as the target it evaluates is actually governed:** while ADR-0015 remains `Proposed`, the target this ADR evaluates is itself only proposed, and this ADR's own acceptance does not, by itself, authorize planning against that target or otherwise substitute for ADR-0015's separate acceptance — a complete bounded implementation-readiness conclusion requires both. This is why the intended milestone review accepts ADR-0015 and this ADR together, rather than treating either one's acceptance alone as sufficient for implementation-readiness purposes. This ADR proposes to resolve the one question ADR-0015's own "Replay/Freshness Boundary" section left open for that target's authorization replay/freshness — whether the target's topology depends on the broader authorization-facing properties that section says remain open — without narrowing, resolving, or presupposing an answer to those broader properties themselves for any other topology, and without resolving the separate operational duplicate-execution/idempotency question either, consistent with what that section already states. This ADR does not change ADR-0015's status.

## Non-Goals

This ADR does not: design generalized replay protection or a replay database; introduce a nonce, TTL, wall-clock expiry value, or signed execution artifact; define distributed or cross-process freshness; define a cross-process or portable authorization-to-execution artifact; define a persistent execution grant; design network-level replay protection generally; design production credential handling; design a caller-facing retry policy, an idempotency key, or an operation-level deduplication mechanism to resolve operational duplicate-execution risk; resolve operational duplicate-execution/idempotency risk at all; authorize or design automatic retry, automatic redispatch, or caller-transparent retry orchestration for the bounded reference implementation, or decide when or whether a subsequent dispatch attempt should ever be initiated after an uncertain outcome; authorize bounded execution implementation of any kind; create an implementation plan, a PR sequence, or a milestone count; publish a `basis-schemas` contract; modify `basis-producer`, `basis-gateway`, `basis-adapters`, `basis-core`, `basis-identity`, or `basis-schemas`; create `basis-executor` or any other new repository; choose a real OT protocol; choose a physical execution target; change ADR-0011's, ADR-0012's, ADR-0013's, ADR-0014's, or ADR-0015's status; mark ADR-0013, ADR-0014, or ADR-0015 `Accepted`; perform the ADR-0013/ADR-0014/ADR-0015 milestone acceptance review; or mark itself `Accepted`. Any class, module, function, or API name that might appear in a future implementation against the ADR-0015 target is not specified by this ADR.

## What This ADR Deliberately Leaves Open

This ADR does not decide: **operational duplicate-execution/idempotency risk** — whether a freshly authorized retry can duplicate an unconfirmed prior attempt's real-world effect — for the ADR-0015 target or any other; a caller-facing retry policy or a maximum retry count; whether, when, or how another dispatch attempt should ever be initiated after an `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or other uncertain outcome — this ADR requires only that the bounded reference implementation not decide that automatically, not what should decide it instead; wall-clock expiry for any topology that does depend on it; policy-reload invalidation semantics generally; restart-surviving replay prevention for any topology that persists a binding artifact; a replay database or its shape; the replay/freshness posture (authorization or operational) of a future separated-executor topology, a future real-OT-protocol target, or any target that introduces a protocol/device credential, persistence, or a transferable artifact; Gate 2's or Gate 3's acceptance; an implementation plan for the ADR-0015 target; or whether the mechanism-by-mechanism reasoning method this ADR applies should become a standing architectural review checklist for future targets — that would itself be a future governance decision, not one this ADR makes.

## Validation / Implementation Gate

This ADR's merging does not itself constitute acceptance, consistent with this repository's established convention (see ADR-0011's through ADR-0015's own Validation / Implementation Gate sections and [`docs/adr/README.md`](README.md#lifecycle-states)) that merging an ADR does not by itself change its status to `Accepted`. Formal acceptance of this ADR, when and if it occurs through a separate, dedicated architecture and governance review, would establish the authorization replay/freshness posture defined here — that the ADR-0015 bounded target does not depend on broader authorization replay/freshness properties, under the constraints stated in **The Boundary of This Conclusion** — as the governed posture for that target, **conditional on that target itself being governed: this ADR's acceptance alone does not make the ADR-0015 target governed architecture, and does not by itself authorize planning against it, if ADR-0015 has not also been separately accepted (Relationship to ADR-0015, above)**; it would not, by itself, authorize implementation. This posture addresses authorization replay/freshness only: acceptance of this ADR would not resolve, and this ADR does not claim to resolve, operational duplicate-execution/idempotency risk, which would remain open exactly as ADR-0012 and ADR-0013 already left it. Acceptance would, however, establish as part of that posture that the first bounded reference implementation must perform at most one dispatch attempt per authorization-to-execution cycle and must not include automatic retry, automatic redispatch, or caller-transparent retry orchestration — the specific condition this ADR's operational-duplicate-execution non-blocking finding depends on; it would not decide when, or whether, a distinct, separately authorized subsequent attempt should ever occur. Gate 2 (ADR-0013) and Gate 3 (ADR-0014) remain outstanding regardless of this ADR's status, pending their own separate, dedicated acceptance reviews; Gate 4 remains conditional and unaffected by this ADR. Acceptance of this ADR would not authorize bounded execution implementation, would not mark ADR-0013, ADR-0014, or ADR-0015 `Accepted`, and would not itself constitute the milestone acceptance review those three ADRs still require. No implementation is authorized by this ADR's proposal, and none would be authorized by its acceptance alone. Protocol execution remains entirely unimplemented and unauthorized.
